The Temporal Ability Channel

Every temporal feature in this repo is a scalar summary of a track. Nothing looks at the shape of a signal over time — and both surviving false-positive classes live there. This is the build that opens that space.

§1 Where this starts

The 26-clip one-agent-per-clip demo corpus is scanned: 713 grouped events, zero labels. The ranked gallery built from it failed, and the two sentences about it are the sharpest result on the corpus and the thing this document is built around:

  • “a lot of those examples are still essentially fragments of viewcone or tiny cracks in the minimap” — the top band;
  • “all the weakest examples shown are either literally only player icons or very close to player icons” — the bottom band.

Together those split the four ranking signals rather than condemning them. self_icon_dist works: it transferred from two Cypher clips to twenty-three agents and cleanly isolates the player-icon band. What fails is the harder cut — abilities against cone fragments and cracks — which lifetime, onset and geometry class carry no information about.

So the organising principle here is not “more features”. It is: every technique must name the false-positive class it attacks, and both surviving classes are layers we can already model. Stop classifying blobs; explain the frame.

§3 The substrate, and why it comes first

Every technique in §4 needs the same thing: a per-frame series at a position. Nothing in this repo produces one. scan_ability_clip keeps a representative dict from the onset frame and three scalars; ability_cone.features_at reads exactly one frame per row. That is the whole reason the temporal space is untried — not that the ideas are hard, but that the data shape does not exist.

Three constraints on that shape, each inherited from a mistake already recorded here:

Keyed on (t_ms, x, y), recomputed — never re-scanned

This is label_rows()'s lesson applied to time. The label store's key is unstable, so re-scanning a labelled session orphans its answers — and the four sessions carrying every ability label the player has ever given are exactly the ones a rescan would destroy. A recompute-keyed design reaches all 254 of them; a rescan-keyed design reaches none, and cannot be scored at all.

Sequential decode, one pass per clip

Both existing tools call cap.set(POS_FRAMES) per sample. For windows that is thousands of seeks, and OpenCV's frame-exact seeking on H.264 is not reliable — which matters quietly and badly when the measurement is a frame-to-frame difference. CLAUDE.md already states the general form of this: seeking is expensive in H.264 and contiguous decoding is cheap — decode ranges, not scattered frames.

Native rate, not the scan's 150 ms

The candidate scan samples at 6.7 Hz. Animation cycles and Tejo's pulses can be faster than that, and at 6.7 Hz they alias into what looks like broadband noise — which would make the animation feature measure the opposite of what it is for. The corpus is 27 clips of 20–64 s at 60 fps, about 18 minutes total; decoding all of it is affordable. Track-building may stay at 150 ms; the series must not inherit it.

§4 Build order

Phase 0prereq

Two repairs, both small

self_icon_dist as a minimum over the track's frames, not the single sample at its birth (scan_ability_clip.py, the branch that opens a track). It is null on 25 of Astra's 43 rows and 58% corpus-wide, while self_rings finds the player in 70–100% of frames — so the nulls are not a detection failure. They are correlated: a candidate is disproportionately born in a frame where something covers the widget, which is exactly when the self colour is hidden. The one sample lands at the worst possible moment. This is the only filter the reading of the gallery confirms works, and its coverage bug is the highest-value repair available.

Per-state standard deviation into the geometry npz. The divisor NOTES has named for a fortnight. Critically not overall SD — at a two-state pixel that measures the lighting switch rather than the noise, so it would be largest precisely on the swept floor where everything interesting lives. sd_lo and sd_hi, computed inside two_state_gray's existing split, from the same frames lo/hi came from.

All 27 demo clips + the Brimstone session share ONE borrowed geometry; the labelled small-widget sessions share another. Three rebuilds cover everything.

Phase 1enabling

prototypes/ability_series.py — the per-frame series

One sequential pass per clip. Given query positions from either the candidate file or the label file, it returns for each position an array aligned over frames: the patch's grayscale, the dark/bright interval excursions, whether the fitted self-cone covers that pixel, the self track's position, and whether detect fired there. One npz per clip, written and dropped — the corpus never sits in memory at once.

Phase 2needs labels

The features, each named by the class it attacks

Detail in §5. Scored against existing labels as a falsification gate only — see §6 on why that cannot validate anything.

Phase 3agreed

The labelling pass, moved to after Phase 1

NOTES put this before the temporal work, on the correct reasoning that everything else is unfalsifiable without it. the player moved it after Phase 1 on 2026-09-04, and the argument is that Phase 1 builds the pass's instrument: a rendered time-series strip per event is far easier to judge than a static crop. That is the labelling-pass skill's own lesson — the tile is not the object — extended one axis. The pass gets cheaper and more accurate for waiting, and the time is the scarce resource.

Invoke the labelling-pass skill first. Run review_candidates.py before launching anything — that gate exists because a labeller was twice launched on garbage.

§5 The features

Cone-coverage conditional

kills → tiny cracks in the minimap

A crack is invisible on unlit ground and becomes visible when the cone sweeps over it, so it acquires a false onset at a fixed position, correlated with cone presence. That is why the onset signal failed to drop them, and it means the discriminator is not a shape feature at all.

Correction to the technique as first written

The plain correlation — does appearance track cone coverage — will also flag real abilities. ability_cone.py already measured that 6 of 22 positives (27%) are invisible to the interval test at their own pixel, because ink on lit ground need only be darker than lit floor. A device that only shows when lit correlates with the cone exactly like a crack does.

The form that survives is two-factor: compare P(visible | covered, t < t₀) against P(visible | covered, t ≥ t₀). A crack is the same before and after; an ability is absent, then present. This uses the onset the scan already stores, and it is strictly stronger than the correlation.

Frames with no cone fit must be dropped from both conditionals and never folded into “not covered” — the fit fails precisely when the widget is occluded, which is when candidates are born. Distinct from ability_cone.py, which used the cone as a per-pixel reference level and did not beat the plain interval residual.

Intra-patch temporal variance

kills → lit static cracks · promotes → animated ultimates

the animation observation turned into a positive feature. A crack, once lit, is static — near-zero variance within its own patch. An animating ultimate is high. It separates exactly the two classes lifetime cannot, and it corrects a known backwardness: an animating icon tracks poorly, fragments, and is then scored down by the lifetime feature, when fragmentation was evidence of a real ability all along.

This feature and the one above interact — score them together

A patch straddling the cone's moving edge has enormous variance, and cone edges are where the other false-positive class lives. So variance alone demotes cracks while promoting cone fragments, and measured independently each would look like a success. Subtract the cone-state expectation first, and report the patch's cone-transition count beside the variance.

Position against the self track

reclassifies → player-anchored overlays

Anything player-anchored moves with him: the drone HUD overlay, the audio ring, Vyse's ultimate. Correlating a candidate's position series against the shipped self track marks those derivable rather than detectable — draw them, do not look for them — and it kills an entire class all four ranking signals were blind to. Two instances of this pattern are already recorded, which makes it a pattern rather than a special case.

Use the median and spread of |p − self| over frames, not a correlation coefficient: a single teleport or dash wrecks a correlation, and Yoru, Omen, Chamber, Veto and Waylay are all in this corpus.

Repeat structure at a position

fixes → pulse fragmentation

How many separate onsets occur at one pixel across a clip. A placed device: one. A crack: one per cone sweep. A pulsing ability (Tejo's E and X): a regular period. Run-length count of on-runs plus an autocorrelation peak.

This pays twice — it also closes ability_corpus.load_events's documented gap, where a pulsing region gets one event per pulse because each pulse is a fresh onset.

Adaptive background

last

A running-window median rather than one over the whole clip, so a long-lived ability eventually becomes background while a crack always is. Deferred deliberately, and for a reason beyond “less obviously right”: it fights two_state_gray, which is the model that made the current detector work, and a running window over a 40-second clip has very few samples in it. Measure it; do not assume it.

§6 What can honestly be scored, and when

The existing labels — 254 rows over six sessions, roughly 95 named positives across five ability classes from three agents — can falsify any feature here today, via label_rows() and recompute. They cannot validate one. That is the same narrow population whose failure to transfer killed the ranked corpus, and repeating the measurement with better features would repeat the mistake with more confidence.

So Phase 2 produces a kill-list, not a precision figure, and no number from it is quotable as corpus precision. Three label-free scores are honest, and all three should run:

scorewhat it measureswhere it comes from
tray-anchored recalldoes a surviving event sit near a known C/Q/E cast§2 — free, per clip, with slot identity
Jett / Neon floorthe per-clip false-positive ratetheir kits draw little or nothing on the widget
clusters vs kit sizeprecision loss, visible without a single labelsurviving events should collapse into about as many distinct places as the agent has drawing abilities

The third is new, and it works because of how the corpus was recorded: one agent per clip, casting deliberately, often repeating. Too many clusters is precision loss you can see directly.

§8 Adjacent threads this opens

  • Ultimate voicelines. Sharpened by §2: X is the one slot the tray cannot read, in 7 of 7 clips. A fixed per-agent voiceline is a closed set, long, loud and spectrally distinctive, needs no minimap — so it reaches ultimates that draw nothing — and the demo clips are a clean reference source at known cast times.
  • Slot order as a prior. the player recorded in C→Q→E order and the tray confirms it independently from the pixels. An event's ordinal position in a clip carries identity information before anything is classified.
  • Ability descriptions. Still the highest value-per-minute input available: a paragraph of the prose constrains the class list harder than a minute of footage, because it says what an object is rather than what it looked like once.

And the calibration that ranks above all of it: uncertainty is acceptable. The deliverable is an event log, not a complete ontology. “An ability icon appeared at (x, y) at t, class unknown” is a usable event. Detect, then localise, then classify — emitting what is known at each level rather than waiting for the level above.
