Reticle · ability channel · 2026-09-05 · supersedes the Phase 2 plan

Ability Recognition, Rebuilt

The labelling pass tripled the corpus and the detector got worse. Not noisier — worse, and measurably so. This document records what died, explains why the weighted combiner was the right last experiment inside the old framing, and sets out what to build instead.

§1 What the pass returned

Five sessions were labelled by hand — Killjoy, Sova, Viper, Skye, and Jett as a deliberate false-positive floor. 161 records, 58 new positives, which with the older sessions gives 85 positives against 252 negatives over 9 sessions, up from 27 positives over 4.

The pass was queued to unblock a weighted combiner. It did something more useful: it falsified the numbers the channel was being steered by.

Every session below chance is an infinite-abilities custom-game demo clip on Ascent. Every session above it is an ordinary capture, and two of those sit on a different source_profile entirely. The feature's failure is perfectly confounded with capture regime, so this corpus cannot distinguish two very different stories:

  • patch_range never generalised, and 0.86 was a four-session artifact of one regime;
  • an infinite-abilities clip is a different visual world — util is being spammed continuously, so the negatives plausibly sit on real ability residue, and intra-patch contrast stops discriminating for a reason that says nothing about ordinary play.

Both readings fit every number here. Only one session of ordinary capture, labelled the new way, separates them — and that is a footage ask, not a code task.

§2 The combiner failed, and how it failed is the finding

prototypes/ability_combiner.py fits L2 logistic regression by Newton–IRLS, with lambda chosen by an inner leave-one-session-out loop over the training sessions only, scored leave-one-session-out on 62 device positives against 240 negatives.

configurationcombiner AUCequal-weight z-sum
6 features, per-session z0.690.74
10 features, per-session z0.700.74
6 features, global z0.640.64

The equal-weight z-sum matches or beats the fitted model in every configuration. The old note predicted it would “tie at best”; it wins.

Measured 2026-09-05 — this is not fold noise, which is what makes it conclusive

Sign agreement across all eight folds is 100% on every feature, and the magnitudes barely move between folds. The fit is not thrashing; it finds a stable, reproducible weighting. That weighting simply carries no advantage over weighting everything equally. A combiner that cannot beat equal weights while fitting consistently is telling you the features are near-redundant — they are re-measuring one quantity, so there is no weighting left to discover.

Which is what the bank looks like when read as a whole. dark_p90, dark_med, dark_max, dark_tvar, onset_step, offset_step, patch_range, autocorr1, persist, life_frac, detect_frac, longest_run, n_runs — thirteen scalar summaries of one grey series at one pixel. They differ in how they summarise, not in what they observe. Adding a fourteenth, or learning what to multiply them by, cannot add an observation.

Measured 2026-09-05 — the biggest lever found all day is not a weighting

Per-session z-scoring is worth 0.74 against 0.64 AUC on the identical equal-weight model — a larger gain than any combiner produced. It uses no labels, so it leaks nothing. That the dominant recoverable variance is per-session rather than per-object is the whole diagnosis: the detector spends most of its discriminative power fighting how a session looks, not what an ability is. Any successor must be session-invariant by construction, not by adding a feature that happens to be robust.

ring inverts self_d_med against device — 0.83 against 0.44 — exactly as the geometry demands, because a radius ring's perimeter is at a radius. smoke is the opposite of a device on contrast and on darkness. These are not noisy versions of one class; they are three shapes.

The distinction that generated the split is the, made while labelling: a radius ring is what a deployed device draws, not the device. Only some kits have one — Killjoy, Chamber, Veto. A candidate that lands on the perimeter sits tens of pixels from the object it belongs to, so every patch feature computed there describes the ring. That is the tile is not the object, one level out, and it currently affects 18 of 62 device-adjacent positives.

Held placeables are a second axis rather than a class: the same ability renders differently while you are holding it and walking around. It has been leaking into category names as place_color and as smoke versus smoke_deployed. It wants a mode field, and it has a measurable consequence — a placement preview sits at the player's feet by definition, so it contaminates self_d_med, the feature whose whole claim is that abilities sit close to the player.

needs labels  ring and place exist in one session each, so their rows above are in-sample and cannot be validated leave-one-session-out. Chamber or Veto footage gives ring a second session; Reyna's Leer and Cypher's Trapwire and Spycam are held placeables already labelled in older sessions, so place may be recoverable from data in hand.

§4 The redirection: stop scoring candidates, start explaining casts

Every technique in the old plan answers is this pixel an ability? — a candidate at a time, independent of everything else in the frame. That framing is now measured out: thirteen views of one pixel's brightness, near-redundant, beaten by their own unweighted sum, and dominated by session appearance.

The evidence points at a different question. The pipeline already knows when abilities were cast, for free, and has never used it.

Measured 2026-09-04, in the document this one supersedes

The HUD ability tray gives cast times by charge-drop on the demo corpus: seven clips, every one clean, zero SUSPECT rows, and the drops fall in C → Q → E slot order — the recording protocol the player described, recovered independently from pixels. It is high precision, low recall, carries slot identity, and costs no labelling time.

That inverts the problem. Instead of asking whether an unexplained blob is an ability, ask: a Killjoy Alarmbot was cast at 26.75s — what appeared on the minimap, and where? Detection with a strong prior on time, agent, slot and — through the reference harvest — deployment shape, health, and icon. The reference already carries 121 abilities across 29 agents with a Deployment Type per ability, which is the class list and the shape family in one.

Three properties make this the right pivot rather than a nicer framing:

  • it is session-invariant by construction. A cast time is read from HUD structure, not from minimap appearance, so it does not care how a session's floor renders — which is the axis §2 says is eating the current detector;
  • it turns precision into a search problem — though not as cheaply as this document first claimed. See the correction below: the narrower question helps, but it does not make the position free;
  • it produces the event the endstate actually wants. CLAUDE.md is explicit that the minimap is instrumental and duels and rounds are the unit. A cast with a time, a slot and an agent is already an event. A scored pixel is not, and never becomes one without this join.

Measured — the search window, and what the long tail actually is

Signed gap from each labelled instance to the nearest cast of its own ability: 5th percentile −2.2s, median +2.9s, 95th +11.0s. Read neither tail as a detector property.

The negative tail is placement hold time, and it is unbounded — a player can hold a placeable for an entire round, so the observed −4.1s is a fact about how long the player happened to hold it, not about the ability. Only the ~1–2s before the commit carries positional information; earlier than that the preview is wherever he was walking. A long hold is not noise to widen a window for — it is its own signal: hesitation, or a player repositioning a placement.

The positive tail is device lifetime. Alarmbot's six labels run +1.9s to +14.7s because a persistent device keeps being labelled for as long as it exists. Both bounds belong to the ability, not the detector.

Superseded within the hour — “slot X never drops” was a reader defect

the player: for ult the tray cast should be the pips going hollow. Rendered and then measured, slot X goes 909 teal pixels → 0 between 28.0s and 28.5s against a first Hunter's Fury label at 28.2s. The pips do not merely hollow — they desaturate along with the bar, so the existing teal mask reads them with no new geometry. Two bugs hid it: casts() looped range(3), and drawn() refused the frame because after the ult every slot is empty — which it read as “tray not rendered”. That is the hp-as-a-death-signal defect from the other side, and an all-spent tray is exactly how a demo clip ends.

Where an ult is still unmatched the cause is the recording, not the widget: Brimstone's clip had infinite abilities on, and Viper's Pit is sustained — the bar holds full while the pit is up and releases only when it ends, landing the drop at 44.0s against labels at 36.9–42.6s. A toggled ult gives a cast time at its end.

Corrected by building it — a cast buys the event, not the position

This document argued that inside a known cast window the candidate set would be small enough for a weak per-pixel score to suffice. Measured, it is not. Taking the candidate whose onset is nearest the cast:

Widening never raised the number of correct picks — it stayed at 3 — so the rule was only ever right where there was nothing to choose between. It is gone. Position is emitted only when the window holds exactly one candidate (2 of 2 exact, 0 px), and is otherwise null with the candidates carried in the event for a later scorer.

So time and identity come free from HUD structure; location still needs the detector that is weak. That belongs beside the 63% wherever it is quoted. It does not undermine the pivot — an event with a time, an agent and an ability is still the thing stage 03 wants — but it does mean §2's exhausted bank is still on the critical path for where, just no longer for whether.

still true  Audio remains the channel for other players' casts, which the tray can never see — and the reference harvest's 56 isolated ultimate voicelines, in ally and enemy variants, are the reference cuts a matched filter was blocked on. the framing is why that generalises rather than merely extends: audio range is roughly the observable range, and roughly what is worth recording, so a supervisor bounded by what can be heard is bounded by what matters.

§5 Two channels that are genuinely untouched

Distinct from the bank in §2, because neither is a summary of grey level at a pixel.

Measured 2026-09-05 — the tint is real and the colour test structurally cannot see it

Killjoy's turret cone is green-tinted. Only 2 of 27 candidates in that window carry colour: green, and colour_frac has median 0.000 across uncertain, positive and negative alike. minimap_dynamic.blob_colour gates on s > 90 and v > 80 before naming any colour, then needs 8% coverage. A translucent tint over floor is low-saturation by construction and never clears 90. The gate was built for solid team-coloured icons and is being asked about illumination.

Do not loosen COLOUR_SAT — its docstring records 90 as load-bearing for keeping achromatic pixels out. This wants a separate low-saturation hue test, which is a new observation rather than a recombination of old ones.

the domain fact, 2026-09-05 — the player is not the only cone source

Controllable deployables emit vision cones too: Sova's Owl Drone, Tejo's Stealth Drone, Skye's Trailblazer and Guiding Light, Fade's Prowler, and Killjoy's turret. the player expects the same half-angle and says he is not sure — so it is measured, never assumed. A turret's arc is a property of the device rather than a player's FOV, and is the case most likely to differ.

This bears directly on the best feature in the bank. cone_cond is computed against self_cone — the player's cone, and only ever the player's. A device lit by its own drone is detected while the player's cone reads dark, which drives the feature the wrong way on exactly the candidates it exists to rescue. the sharper version: a turret placed in a doorway lights a wedge the player's own cone never covers. The per-session numbers are consistent with that, and were predicted before they were computed:

Skye     0.08   dog AND bird       Sova    0.29   owl drone
Killjoy  0.17   turret             Viper   0.31   no cone deployable

not proof  Four sessions, and the mechanism is not isolated — but the direction and the ordering are both as predicted, and the fix is a mechanism to build rather than a weight to tune.

Two further notes on cones, both cheap and both currently unused. Ally cones need no ally identity across frames, because a cone is a per-frame quantity — position and facing in that frame — and minimap.ally_rings() already returns the (area, cx, cy) tuple self_cone() takes as a seed. Ring-seeding is what took cone yield from 8% to 89% pooled, and allies give rings. But the demo corpus is solo, so ally cones buy nothing there and everything on real-match footage; deployable cones are the reverse.

Found 2026-09-05 — 88 positives are stranded, which is more than the entire usable corpus

a06f04a0059f (53 positives) and 5822b6646448 (35) were labelled positives-only — zero negatives each — so the scorer skips them for having no both-class labels, silently. Labelling negatives on those two more than doubles the corpus, needs no new footage and no new video decode, and brings in all the Deadlock held-placeable data that §3 wants. 5822b6646448 additionally dies on a stale npz schema and is not on the never-re-scan list, so it can simply be rebuilt.

The second is the regime confound in §1. Until one ordinary-capture session is labelled the new way, every figure here is measured across two visual worlds at once, and a model fitted on it can fit the world instead of the ability. That is the one footage ask in this document, and it is worth more than any number of additional demo clips.
