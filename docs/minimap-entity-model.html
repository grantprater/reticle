The Minimap Entity Model

Every stall in this channel came from asking “what is this blob?”. The domain facts the player has given are not about appearance — they describe a small set of entities with parameters, and something that drives each parameter. This is that model, and the test it has to pass.

§1 What this has to earn

A model of this kind is cheap to write and expensive to be wrong about. The project's own judgement log is blunt on the point: claims at the ontology level score 0.38 accuracy with a −0.46 confidence gap and the highest cost of any class — the two wrong ones on record cost a session each. And the standing instruction is the right brake: do not gate the event log on the taxonomy; an unresolved class is a cost, not a fault.

So this document is only worth having if it predicts something the current code gets wrong. §8 is that list, and every row there is either already measured or falsifiable with data in hand. If those rows don't hold, the model is decoration and should be deleted rather than refined.

§2 The entity

One record describes anything drawn on the widget that is not the map itself. The fields are ordinary; the third column is the whole idea.

A point. Where the thing is, or where it started.

How far it reaches from the origin: nothing (a point), a length along the bearing (a wall, a bolt), or a radius (a smoke, a ring). Extent is what animates.

The entity this one belongs to, if any. A radius ring belongs to a device; a cone belongs to an emitter; a placement preview belongs to the device it becomes. Anchored entities are derived, not detected — §5.

Map-space or screen-space. A drone's HUD wedge sits inside the minimap ROI without being minimap content, and today it gets a confident world position through the homography that means nothing.

Every field is three-valued, and two of the three are not the same

A value; absent by construction (the ability has no such parameter — a smoke's bearing, an ability that draws nothing at all); or unknown by refusal (the measurement could not answer). The first is information and complete. The second is a gap. Collapsing them is how a gap gets quoted as a fact, which is the failure this repo has paid for more than once — and it is why LOBE_MIN_FRAC exists, so a perfect circle cannot report a bearing out of noise.

§3 The claim: a driver attaches to a parameter, not to an object

This is the load-bearing part of the model and the one worth attacking first.

A flat per-object driver table was tried in ability_cast.py and handled Killjoy's turret badly, because the turret is not “static” or “reactive” — it is both, in different parameters. Its origin never moves. Its bearing snaps onto an enemy in its detection cone. One label cannot carry that; two fields can.

Any scheme that labels an object rather than its parameters loses that distinction, and with it the thing that makes the motion worth reading at all.

The consequence that is worth more than the schema

CLAUDE.md lists opponent priors as blocked, for now: needs enemy positions, which no capture of one's own screen contains. But an alarmbot whose origin is enemy-driven is moving toward an enemy, and a turret whose bearing is enemy-driven is pointing at one. Your own utility is an enemy sensor, at a known position and time, needing no new extractor — just the position track applied to an object the cast already identified.

untestable here The demo corpus is solo, so an enemy-reactive parameter has nothing to react to. the player, directly: the kj ones wouldn't move because there were no enemies. This needs match footage, and absence of motion in these clips is not evidence.

§4 Existence is a set of intervals

The natural assumption is that an ability is placed, lives, and dies. on Viper: she has a resource bar, so she can put her smoke and screen up and down throughout the round — and pick the smoke up and redeploy it. Vyse's flash flower was already recorded with the same property.

So three things that look like defects are correct behaviour:

  • a device disappearing does not mean it was destroyed. It may be down, or picked up;
  • the same entity can reappear later, somewhere else. Identity persists across a gap and the origin changes with it — so a redeployment is one entity with two origins, not two entities;
  • lifetime features are measuring the wrong thing on these. n_observations and duration_ms — the pair that doubled precision — see one entity's on/off cycles as several short-lived objects. That is a caveat on the best-performing feature in the bank, and it applies to exactly the abilities a Viper or Vyse brings.

It also changes what the tray can tell you for one agent: a resource bar is a level, not a charge count, so Viper's casts are drains and refills rather than the discrete drops ability_hud.py looks for. Her Pit already behaves differently for a related reason — it is sustained, and the bar releases when the pit ends rather than when it starts.

The practical payoff of naming the edge is that an anchored entity stops needing to be found. Draw what should be there, subtract, and the residual is either an unmodelled entity or an error. Detection and validation from one mechanism, which is the same move that made the two-state background work.

§6 The drivers are tracking constraints

Recorded 2026-09-06, stating the direction this is headed:

“identity-based identification of entities with their temporal evolution, subject to the invariants of their specific identity (most agents cannot teleport or dash, certain abilities have certain durations, movement characteristics, etc.)”

That is what §3's driver column is for, and the document did not say so. A driver is not a description of how a thing behaves — it is the law that thing's track has to obey. Identity comes first; the identity carries a set of invariants; the invariants constrain what the next frame may contain.

One motion model is the wrong shape, and that was the first proposal

The plan for ally identity said “constant-velocity Kalman with gating and Hungarian assignment”. As a single model that is wrong, and this section is why: it would fight the truth for exactly the entities that matter. A Cypher cam rotates and never translates. A teleport is a legal discontinuity for five agents. A ping is static with a known lifetime. One prior treats all three as tracking faults.

The right structure is identity-conditional dynamics — a motion model per identity class, with association scoring a candidate under the model its hypothesised identity implies. The classical name is interacting-multiple-model / multiple-hypothesis tracking, so this is prior art to read rather than re-derive.

The inventory below is the point. None of it is speculative and none of it needs new footage — it is already written down across this repo, and until now nothing consumed it.

Transient overlap is what makes this tractable — measured 2026-09-06

for truly overlapping icons, in the vast majority of cases there was probably an instant where they were not overlapping. Tested on 587c15b07779 over ±3 s windows: the extent of the object at an event's position varies by a median factor of 4.75 (range 1.60–51.62), 9 of 10 events exceed 3×, and a median 12% of sampled frames offer a clean look. Rendered, two ally icons are one object at onset and two objects 0.3 s later.

This is a third source of separation after appearance and constraints — and widget_objects.py had already measured that appearance gives nothing, since no per-object shape feature separates a ping from an ally at any grouping distance. So the tracker is not an optimisation of the grouping pass, it is a precondition for it.

The circularity this creates, named before it bites

Identity constrains the track, and the track's behaviour is evidence for identity. Left unseparated that is the seeding mistake in a fourth costume — scoring a hypothesis against itself, which has cost this repo three times (minimap_agent, plant_probe's controls, answer()'s refusal of claude-* provenance).

The discipline: identity comes from an independent source wherever one exists — the scoreboard lineup, the tray's cast times, the killfeed, the roster — and track behaviour is used to falsify an identity, never to assign one. “This translated 200 px, so it is not a Cypher cam” is sound. “This did not move, so it is a cam” is the trap.

The first test it has to pass, and it needs no new data

prototypes/CLAUDE.md quotes jumps over 60 px/s at 5.0% / 3.3% / 3.8% across the three geometry-validated sessions, read as the track's residual error rate — and already records that the figure conflates tracking error, teleports and dashes, of which only the first is a defect. Under identity-conditional dynamics that single number splits three ways, and two of the three stop being errors. It should fall on a session where a mobility agent was played, without touching the detector. If it does not, this section is decoration.

§7 What the model already predicted correctly

Measured 2026-09-05 — extent along the bearing

If a wall's fragments are an extending extent rather than one object that grew, they should share a bearing from the origin with distance increasing over time. Viper's Toxic Screen: the current onset rule splits its window into 5 objects; grouping by bearing returns +10° ×3 — the three collinear fragments — and correctly rejects the two candidates at −96° and −61°. Sova's Hunter's Fury returns +46° ×4 and +78° ×3, two bolt directions from one origin, which is what that ultimate does. Across all 12 multi-candidate windows, objects fall from 49 to 30. prototypes/ability_extent.py.

read the caveat The rule was designed after looking at the Toxic Screen window, so that case is the one it was fitted to, not evidence for it. The other eleven are unvalidated, and at a 15° tolerance a random pair agrees about 8% of the time.

The gap this exposed, and it is cheap to close

Nothing in the label store says which candidates are one object. Grouping has been in this pipeline since ability_corpus and has never been scored against anything. One labelling pass — the player marking fragments that belong together — would settle the bearing rule, the onset rule, and the position refusals in ability_cast at once.

§9 What to do with this

Not a rewrite. The model is a notation for facts already established, and its value is in what it stops — so introduce it exactly where a row of §8 is costing something.

  • Build the identity-conditional tracker first. Re-ordered 2026-09-06: overlap is transient, so a grouping pass over single frames asks the hardest available version of its question. The tracker is the precondition, and the pass should then present a window and be answered at the instant the objects are apart;
  • Label which candidates are one object, on top of that. It remains the cheapest pass available and it unblocks every grouping question at once;
  • split driver across parameters in the ability event, rather than one label per object. The flat version was already measured as too coarse for a turret;
  • type the frame, so a screen-space overlay can never receive a world position. This is a correctness bug with a known cause and no fix;
  • leave the rest as prose until a §8 row makes it pay. An ontology built ahead of its evidence is the failure this document opened by quoting.
